People usually approach this as a headcount question. It is not, quite. A household of four where one person handles every payment behaves differently from a household of two where both transact constantly.

## The question that actually decides it

**How many people in your home can move money or approve something?**

If the answer is one, an Individual plan is generally the right shape. If it is more than one — a spouse with their own accounts, a teenager with UPI, a parent whose phone receives the OTPs — you are looking at a household exposure, and Family cover is designed for that.

## What changes with a household

Three things, and none of them are about the number of people.

**Shared devices.** A tablet used by everyone means one compromise reaches everyone's sessions. Household risk is not additive; it is connected.

**Reused credentials.** Families reuse passwords across each other far more than individuals do, usually for practical reasons — shared subscriptions, shared logins, a family email.

**Children and teenagers.** This is the exposure with no individual-plan equivalent. Cyberbullying, harassment, impersonation and in-app spending are household problems, and the cyberbullying section of a policy is typically where Family cover earns its place.

## What does not change

The core sections — unauthorised transactions, identity theft, phishing, extortion, data restoration — appear in both. You are not buying different protections. You are buying protection across more people, and usually one additional section that only matters if there are dependents.

## What we cannot tell you here

Sum insured options, per-member limits, household limits, and the exact definition of a dependent are all set by the insurer. Age limits for dependents vary, and so does whether a plan counts members or simply covers the household.

We will not publish numbers we cannot verify. Those come from the specific policy, on your quote.

## A reasonable default

If you are unsure, start with the honest version of the question: if something happened to the least careful person in your house tomorrow, would you want it covered? If yes, you are looking at Family.

Coverage is subject to the applicable policy terms, conditions, exclusions, limits and underwriting requirements.
