Identity theft is badly named. It suggests a moment — something taken. In practice it is a process, and the part that costs you is not the theft. It is the proving.

## How people find out

Almost never at the time. The common discovery routes are:

- A loan or credit card appears on a credit report you happened to check.
- A collections call arrives about an account you never opened.
- A tax or KYC notice references income or activity that is not yours.
- A bank refuses an application for reasons that make no sense.

By then the account may be months old, and the paperwork trail was built using your documents.

## Why it takes so long to fix

Because the burden of proof sits with you, and it sits with you at every institution separately. A police complaint, correspondence with each bank, disputes with credit bureaus, affidavits, and follow-up on all of it. Each one is individually reasonable and collectively exhausting.

This is also why identity theft has a cost even when no money left your own account. The expense is legal help, documentation, time off work, and sometimes professional assistance to coordinate it.

## What reduces the risk

- **Do not send document scans over chat.** Once an Aadhaar or PAN image is in a WhatsApp thread, you have lost control of where it goes.
- **Check your credit report periodically.** It is the earliest reliable signal, and it is free.
- **Be deliberate about who gets original documents.** Ask why, ask how it will be stored, and prefer masked or redacted copies where accepted.
- **Secure your email.** Almost every account recovery route runs through it.

## Where insurance fits

This is the section of a personal cyber policy that people understand least and benefit from most, because the loss here is largely *cost of recovery* rather than stolen funds.

Depending on the policy, cover may assist with eligible legal costs of restoring your identity and disputing fraudulent accounts, and with certain administrative expenses incurred while resolving the matter. Documentation requirements are substantial, and incidents that began before the policy started are generally excluded.

Coverage is subject to the applicable policy terms, conditions, exclusions, limits and underwriting requirements.
